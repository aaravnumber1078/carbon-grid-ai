Inference that answers to the grid.

CarbonGrid sits between your application and a local language model. For every prompt it scores quantization risk, reads live grid carbon intensity, and runs the model at FP16 or INT4, whichever emits less CO₂ within your latency budget.

Decision engine

Three checks, in a fixed order, on every request.

The policy is small enough to read in one sitting, and that's deliberate. Every response records which check decided it, so any routing choice can be audited after the fact.

  1. 01

    Score quantization risk

    A lightweight classifier reads the prompt and its task type and estimates how likely an INT4 answer is to hold up against FP16. Prompts below the pass mark skip the remaining checks and run at full precision.

    safety_threshold = 0.50
  2. 02

    Respect the latency budget

    If the request sets a time limit, each precision's measured latency is compared against it with a small tolerance. A precision that would miss the budget is ruled out before carbon is considered.

    latency_tolerance = 10%
  3. 03

    Pick the lower-carbon precision

    Estimated CO₂ is the measured energy per answer multiplied by the grid's current carbon intensity. The lower figure wins. Differences under a tenth of a milligram count as a tie and go to the faster precision.

    co2_tie_threshold = 0.1 mg

Guardrails

  • Free GPU memory is checked first. FP16 needs 3,200 MB and INT4 needs 1,400 MB.
  • If the grid API is down, readings come from a replay file, then a fixed 200 g/kWh.
  • If neither precision fits the latency budget, the request falls back to FP16.
  • If the classifier can't run, the request stays on FP16.

API

One endpoint. Every decision explained.

POST a prompt to /generate and get the answer back with its full routing record: the precision chosen and why, the grid reading behind it, and the energy the GPU used while generating it.

  • A machine-readable decision reason on every response
  • Energy measured through NVML, and flagged as estimated when the sensor isn't available
  • Grid source, reading age and staleness flags
  • Free VRAM and per-precision memory thresholds

Results

What 182 prompts on a laptop GPU taught us.

Every prompt ran three ways: always FP16, always INT4, and routed by CarbonGrid. We report the results that didn't go our way alongside the ones that did.

  1. INT4 doesn't save energy on short answers. It draws less power but takes about twice as long, so energy per answer comes out the same as FP16.
  2. Compression costs quality. INT4 answers averaged 0.52 against 1.00 for the FP16 reference.
  3. On the short-answer profile, routing matched FP16. CarbonGrid kept every prompt on FP16, with no significant difference in energy or speed (p = 0.10).
  4. On the mixed workload, routing saved energy. 52% of prompts went to INT4 and energy fell about 2.5% versus always FP16. Answers were 4.3 s slower, and routed INT4 answers scored 0.66 on quality.
  5. CO₂ figures are estimates. They're measured energy multiplied by 200 g/kWh, the offline grid value used during testing.
